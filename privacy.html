Privacy Policy

Pawprint keeps what you add about your dogs on your iPhone. There's no account, no server of ours, no ads and no tracking.

Effective date: 6 November 2026

At a glance

  • On your iPhone only. Dog profiles, health records, photos and 3D scans are saved on your device. The developer never receives them.
  • No account, no sign-in. Pawprint never asks for your name, email or phone number.
  • No tracking, no ads, no analytics. Pawprint contains no third-party SDKs and doesn't use the advertising identifier.
  • You choose what leaves. Things leave your iPhone only when you share them, tap a link, or turn on the optional Private Cloud Compute setting (Deep thinking).
  • Delete any time. Delete a dog and all their records in the app, or delete the app to remove everything.

1. Who this covers

This policy covers the Pawprint app for iPhone, including its Home Screen widget, its Siri and Shortcuts actions, and this website. "We" means Pawprint's developer. Pawprint is free, has no in-app purchases and shows no ads.

Because Pawprint has no account system and no server, we don't collect, see or store any information about you or your dogs. The rest of this page explains what the app keeps on your device and the few cases where something can leave it.

2. What's stored on your iPhone

Everything you enter is saved in a database on your iPhone. Depending on what you choose to fill in, that can include:

  • Dog profiles: name, breed, sex, birthdate, size, colour and markings, neutered status, microchip number and registry, licence number, a profile photo or drawing, allergies, conditions, food and feeding amounts, meal times, activity level, body condition, exercise goal, diet and sitter notes, and other notes.
  • Care contacts: your vet clinic, vet's name, vet and emergency-vet phone numbers, and pet insurance provider and policy number.
  • Records: weights, vaccinations, medications, vet visits (including any cost you enter), care reminders, health notes, meals and walks you log, and the activities you mark done in Georgia's daily plan.
  • Memories: the date your dog came home and, if you choose to mark a dog as in memory, the date they died and any words of remembrance.
  • Photobook photos: a resized copy of each photo you pick, a thumbnail, the date it was taken (read from the photo file when it has one), and your caption and favourites. Pawprint saves a re-encoded copy without the original file's other metadata, such as location.
  • 3D scans: 3D model files (.usdz) of your dog that you capture or import, with a thumbnail and the model's size.
  • Georgia's feeding advice: if you ask for it on a dog's feeding plan, the advice is saved on that dog's profile.
  • Settings: your region (first suggested from your iPhone's region setting), morning briefing time, and Georgia's options.

Georgia, Pawprint's dog-care assistant, works from a library of dog-care articles, food and substance checks and breed profiles built into the app. Her daily plan, insights, food checks and answers are worked out on your iPhone from this library and your dog's details.

3. The widget

Pawprint's database sits in a storage area on your iPhone shared only between the Pawprint app and its own widget (an iOS "app group"). The "Next up" widget reads it to show your dogs' upcoming care tasks, vaccines and follow-ups on your Home Screen. The widget only reads; it doesn't send anything anywhere. Anyone who can see your Home Screen can see what the widget shows, so remove the widget if you'd rather keep that private.

4. Notifications

If you turn on reminders (meal reminders, care reminders, vaccine due dates, vet follow-ups) or Georgia's morning briefing, Pawprint asks for permission to send notifications. They're scheduled on your iPhone; nothing goes through a server of ours. A notification can include your dog's name and the task, for example a meal time and amount, or a vaccine that's due, and may appear on your Lock Screen depending on your iOS settings. You can turn notifications off at any time in the iPhone Settings app, under Notifications, then Pawprint.

5. Camera and photos

Camera

Pawprint asks for camera access for two things, and only when you start them:

  • Food and treat labels. You point the camera at a label and capture it. The text is read on your iPhone using Apple's built-in text recognition, and Georgia checks the ingredients. The label photo isn't kept. If you choose, the calories per cup are saved to your dog's feeding plan.
  • 3D scans. On iPhones that support Apple's Object Capture, you photograph your dog from every side. The photos are kept in a temporary folder while your iPhone builds the 3D model, then deleted. Only the finished model is saved.

You can refuse or later turn off camera access in the Settings app; you can still choose a label photo from your library instead.

Photos

When you add a profile photo, photobook photos or a label photo, Pawprint uses Apple's photo picker. The picker runs outside the app, so Pawprint gets only the photos you select and never has access to the rest of your library. Pawprint doesn't ask for photo library permission.

6. Siri and Shortcuts

Pawprint offers actions you can use with Siri and the Shortcuts app: Mark as Fed, Log a Walk, Today's Plan, Can My Dog Eat This? and Ask Georgia. When you use one, Pawprint reads or updates your data on your iPhone and replies, for example by saying today's plan or whether a food is safe. Apple processes your voice request under Apple's own privacy terms; Pawprint receives only the action and what you asked for, such as the dog's name or the food.

7. Apple Intelligence

Reworded answers (off by default)

Georgia answers in her own reviewed words. In Georgia's settings you can turn on Reword answers with Apple Intelligence. It's off until you turn it on. When it's on, and your iPhone supports Apple Intelligence and has it turned on, Apple's on-device language model may reword some everyday answers. To do that it's given your question, Georgia's notes for the answer and the details about your dog the answer needs. This runs on your iPhone. Georgia checks every reworded answer and shows her own words instead if anything looks wrong. Emergencies, medicines and symptoms always get Georgia's own words.

Feeding advice

On a dog's feeding plan, on iPhones with Apple Intelligence available, you can tap Ask Georgia for (your dog's name)'s advice or type a feeding question. Apple's on-device model then writes advice from your dog's profile and feeding plan. This happens only when you ask, runs on your iPhone, and the advice is saved on the dog's profile.

Deep thinking with Private Cloud Compute (opt-in)

On iOS versions that support it, once reworded answers are on, you can also turn on Deep thinking. It's off until you turn it on. When it's on, longer or more complex questions may be sent, with the details about your dog they need, to Apple's Private Cloud Compute to be worded. Apple runs Private Cloud Compute, not us. Apple says it doesn't store your data or make it available to anyone, including Apple. Turn Deep thinking off and every answer is worked out on your iPhone again.

8. When Pawprint uses the internet

Pawprint has no server and sends nothing to us. It connects to the internet only in these cases:

  • Live source check in Essentials. When you open a product in the Essentials tab, Pawprint checks that the manufacturer's website and the certification registry it links to are online. These are plain web requests to those sites. No information about you or your dogs is included, but like any website visit, those sites can see your device's IP address and the time of the request.
  • Links you tap. Links to article sources, manufacturers, registries and official guidance open in Safari. "Find an emergency vet" opens a search in Apple Maps. Phone numbers open your Phone app. Once you leave Pawprint, those apps and websites have their own privacy policies.
  • Private Cloud Compute, only if you turn on Deep thinking (see section 7).

9. Sharing you start

Some screens have a Share button, for example to share a photobook PDF, a sitter sheet (text or PDF), a health summary for your vet, questions for a vet visit, a library article or a 3D model. Pawprint creates the file or text on your iPhone and opens Apple's share sheet. Nothing is sent until you pick where it goes, such as Messages, Mail, AirDrop or Files. After that, the app or person you shared with handles it.

A web page of your dog. If you choose Share as web page, Pawprint makes one HTML file on your iPhone from your dog's name, breed, birth year (or the exact birthday, if you choose to show it), the date your dog came home, the photobook photos you pick with their captions and dates, and, for a dog you've marked as passed away, the years and the words of remembrance you choose to include. Health records, medicines, vet, microchip and insurance details, sitter notes and your own notes are never included. The photos are re-encoded copies without location or other file metadata. Pawprint doesn't upload or host the page: anyone you send the file to can open it in a browser, and can pass it on, so share it only with people you trust with those photos.

10. What Pawprint doesn't do

  • It doesn't have accounts, sign-in or a server, and doesn't send your data to the developer.
  • It doesn't include third-party SDKs: no analytics, crash reporting, advertising or social login.
  • It doesn't track you across apps or websites, use the advertising identifier, or sell or share your information.
  • It doesn't use your location, contacts, microphone or Health data.
  • It doesn't sync through iCloud.

This website doesn't use cookies, analytics or tracking either. It's hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub, like any web host, may log your IP address and the pages you request; see GitHub's privacy statement. The example dog page carries its own font inside the page and loads nothing from other sites.

11. Backups

If you back up your iPhone with iCloud Backup or to a computer, iOS includes Pawprint's data in that backup, the same as for other apps. Apple, or your computer, handles those backups; we can't access them.

12. Deleting your data

Your data is on your iPhone, so you're in control of deleting it:

  • A record: swipe left on a vaccine, medication, vet visit, reminder or health note in a dog's profile, or on an entry in Weight history, and tap Delete.
  • A photo or 3D scan: open the photo and tap Delete photo, or swipe left on a scan in the dog's 3D scans.
  • A dog: swipe left on the dog in the Dogs tab and confirm. This deletes the dog and all their records, photos and 3D scans.
  • Everything: delete the Pawprint app from your iPhone. This removes all of Pawprint's data, including what the widget shows and any scheduled notifications. Copies in older device backups stay until those backups are replaced or deleted.

We hold no copy, so we can't restore anything you delete.

13. Your rights

Privacy laws such as the GDPR in Europe and the UK, and state laws such as the CCPA in California, give you rights to access, correct, delete and move your personal information, and to object to how it's used. Pawprint's developer doesn't collect or hold any personal information about you, so there's nothing for us to hand over or delete: you can see, change, share and delete everything directly in the app. We don't sell or share personal information. If you have a question about your rights, contact us (section 16).

14. Children

Pawprint is made for people looking after dogs and isn't directed at children. It doesn't collect personal information from anyone, children included, and has no accounts, chat with other people or ads. If a child uses Pawprint on a family iPhone, what they add stays on that iPhone and can be deleted as described in section 12.

15. Changes to this policy

If Pawprint changes how it handles information, for example by adding a feature that sends data off your device, we'll update this page and the effective date at the top.
